The HyperNet trades wide attention capacity for dynamic weight generation — same total budget, different allocation. Baseline spends 63% on attention matrices; HyperNet variants split ~31% attention / 32% HyperNet / 31% FFN. The shared HyperNet (208k params) outputs low-rank ΔW corrections for Q and K every hop: Q′ = Q + n·U·Vᵀ (rank=8).

Key findings
Where ΔW helps
qa1: Mean→ΔW gains +8.1pp mean — driven by seed 44 breaking through to 82.2% (vs baseline 54.1%). The sequence-mean context signal lets the hypernetwork specialise attention per-sample.

qa15: CLS→ΔW seed 42 hit perfect 100%. The deduction task seems uniquely suited to CLS-conditioned weight shifts, though only one seed triggered this mode.

qa19: Mean→ΔW seeds 44 and 46 reached 20.8% and 18.8%, substantially above the baseline ceiling of ~12–14%.
Where ΔW doesn't help
qa2 / qa16: All three models are statistically tied. Two-hop and induction tasks appear to need fundamentally different reasoning beyond what context-conditioned Q/K shifts can provide.

qa3: Only marginal gains (~1pp). Three-hop chaining seems bottlenecked by architecture depth, not attention quality.

Variance problem: ΔW gains are unstable — high σ values (±9.93% for Mean/qa1, ±12.99% for CLS/qa15) mean results are not reproducibly better. The gains are real but seed-sensitive.
